Jellyfin is a media server: it reads your movie and show folders, fetches covers, descriptions and cast from online databases and plays everything on your TV, phone or in the browser. Unlike Plex or Emby, Jellyfin is completely free. There's no account with the vendor and no features behind a paywall.

The most important part of the setup doesn't happen in Jellyfin, but beforehand in the file system. If folders and file names are right, Jellyfin recognizes almost everything by itself. If not, you'll later sort every movie by hand.

<Steps>

<Step title="Create the media folders">
Your media does **not** live in `__ROOT__`. It's big, changes rarely and usually belongs on a disk of its own. Besides, you don't want to back it up every night with the rest of the homelab. In this guide, it lives under `/srv/media`. If you have a second disk mounted at `/mnt/media`, for example, use that path.

Jellyfin likes one library per media type. Create a folder for each:

```bash
sudo mkdir -p /srv/media/Filme /srv/media/Serien
sudo chown -R $USER:$USER /srv/media
```

The examples use the German folder names `Filme` (movies) and `Serien` (shows); feel free to name them `Movies` and `Shows` instead and adjust the paths below. Inside the folders, a fixed structure helps. Jellyfin recognizes the title with the release year in brackets most reliably:

```
/srv/media/
├── Filme/
│   ├── Das Boot (1981)/
│   │   └── Das Boot (1981).mkv
│   └── Metropolis (1927)/
│       └── Metropolis (1927).mp4
└── Serien/
    └── Dark (2017)/
        ├── Season 01/
        │   ├── Dark S01E01.mkv
        │   └── Dark S01E02.mkv
        └── Season 02/
            └── Dark S02E01.mkv
```

What matters most is the year for movies and the `S01E01` pattern for shows. All rules, including music, extras and several versions of a movie, are in the [Jellyfin docs on movies](https://jellyfin.org/docs/general/server/media/movies) and [on shows](https://jellyfin.org/docs/general/server/media/shows).
</Step>

<Step title="Sort out the permissions">
In the container, Jellyfin doesn't run as root but with your user ID. It's set as `PUID` and `PGID` in the `.env`, and the template applies it with `user:` in the `compose.yaml`. For this to work, this user needs:

- **Read** access to your media folder. If you gave it to yourself with `chown` as above, that fits.
- **Write** access to its own data folders for settings and cache.

You create the data folders yourself before the container starts. Otherwise Docker creates them as root, and Jellyfin fails with `Permission denied`:

```bash
mkdir -p __ROOT__/jellyfin __DATA(jellyfin)__/config __DATA(jellyfin)__/cache
```

`id` shows your IDs. For the first user on Debian and Ubuntu, both are usually `1000`. More on the topic under [Permission denied](/en/wiki/permission-denied/).

In the template, the media folder is mounted with `:ro`, read-only. Then Jellyfin can't change or delete anything in your collection. If you later want to store covers and info files right next to the movies, or delete media through the interface, remove the `:ro`.
</Step>

<Step title="Write compose.yaml and .env">
Enter your media folder under `MEDIA_DIR` in the `.env`, for example `/srv/media`. In the container, it's then called `/media`, with the subfolders `/media/Filme` and `/media/Serien`.

<ServiceFiles id="jellyfin" />

Jellyfin needs its own subdomain. Behind a path like `/jellyfin`, it only runs with extra settings.
</Step>

<Step title="Start and initial setup">
```bash
cd __ROOT__/jellyfin
docker compose up -d
docker compose logs -f jellyfin
```

As soon as no new lines appear in the log, open the address in the browser. A wizard walks you through the setup. Finish it right away, because until then, anyone who can reach the page can create the admin account:

1. Choose the interface **language**.
2. Create the **administrator**. Use a user name of your own and a long password from your password manager. This account may do everything.
3. You can create **libraries** here already, or skip this and do it in the next step.
4. Set the **metadata language** and country to yours. Then titles and descriptions come in your language where available.
5. **Remote access**: leave the option for remote connections turned on. Whether the server is really reachable from outside is decided at the reverse proxy and the router, not here.

Afterwards, create accounts for your family under **Dashboard → Users**. Each account remembers what has been watched, and you can limit access per library or by parental rating.
</Step>

<Step title="Create libraries">
Under **Dashboard → Libraries**, add one library each:

| Content type | Folder in the container |
|---|---|
| Movies | `/media/Filme` |
| Shows | `/media/Serien` |

Jellyfin only sees paths inside the container, so `/media/…`, not `/srv/media/…`. You can leave the other settings as they are at first. After saving, Jellyfin scans the folders and fetches metadata. With large collections, the first scan takes a while.

If a title was recognized wrongly, open it, choose **Identify** in the menu and search for the right entry. If this happens often, it's almost always down to folder or file names.
</Step>

<Step title="Connect apps">
In the browser, Jellyfin runs without installation. For other devices there are apps, most of them free:

- **Android and Android TV / Fire TV:** the official Jellyfin app from the respective store.
- **iPhone, iPad, Apple TV:** for example Swiftfin or the Jellyfin app.
- **Computer:** the browser or Jellyfin's desktop app.
- **Smart TVs** from LG and Samsung have apps of their own, somewhat fiddly to install on some models.

The current overview is on [Jellyfin's download page](https://jellyfin.org/downloads/clients). In the app, enter the full address with `https://` as the server and log in with your Jellyfin account.
</Step>

<Step title="Hardware transcoding (optional)">
If a device can't play a video directly, because of the codec or because bandwidth is lacking on the go, Jellyfin converts it in real time. That's called **transcoding**. On the CPU, it costs a lot of performance. Many Intel processors have a dedicated unit for it in the integrated graphics (iGPU): **Quick Sync**. It handles several streams at once with little power. AMD iGPUs work similarly through VA-API.

First check whether the iGPU shows up in the system, and read out the group ID of `render`:

```bash
ls -l /dev/dri
getent group render | cut -d: -f3
```

If you see a device `renderD128`, enter the number from the second command as `RENDER_GID` in the `.env`. Then remove the `#` in front of the `devices:` and `group_add:` lines and their entries in the `compose.yaml`, and restart the container:

```bash
cd __ROOT__/jellyfin
docker compose up -d
docker compose exec jellyfin /usr/lib/jellyfin-ffmpeg/vainfo
```

`vainfo` lists the codecs the iGPU supports. If you get an error instead of a list, usually the device access is missing or the group isn't right.

Finally, turn on hardware acceleration in the **Dashboard** under the **Transcoding** settings. Choose "Intel QuickSync (QSV)" for Intel or "Video Acceleration API (VA-API)" for AMD, and only check the codecs `vainfo` showed. Details for each processor generation are in the [Jellyfin docs on Intel](https://jellyfin.org/docs/general/post-install/transcoding/hardware-acceleration/intel).

<Callout title="Is Jellyfin running in a VM?">
Under Proxmox, the iGPU first has to be passed through to the VM or LXC container before Docker can see it. That's a topic of its own. It's simpler to run Jellyfin directly on the host or in an LXC container.
</Callout>
</Step>

<Step title="Access from outside">
Through your reverse proxy, Jellyfin is reachable with HTTPS. Whether the address also works from the internet depends on how you've set up [access from outside](/en/wiki/access-methods-compared/):

- **VPN** like [WireGuard](/en/setup/vpn-wireguard/): the safest way. Jellyfin stays on the home network, and you connect your phone or laptop through the VPN. Downside: on some TVs and at friends' places, you can't set up a VPN.
- **Port forwarding** to the reverse proxy: works with all apps. Then the login page is public. Use long passwords, don't give any account admin rights without need, and keep Jellyfin up to date.

So Jellyfin sees your devices' real addresses and not just the proxy's, enter the proxy under known proxies in the **Dashboard** under **Networking**, for example with its container name `traefik`. That matters for logs and for separate bitrate limits at home and on the go. The details are in the [Jellyfin docs on reverse proxies](https://jellyfin.org/docs/general/post-install/networking/reverse-proxy/).

Login protection like [Tinyauth](/en/setup/tinyauth/) or [Pocket ID](/en/setup/pocket-id/) as middleware in front of Jellyfin only works in the browser. The apps can't deal with the login page in front.

<Callout type="warning" title="No streaming through Cloudflare Tunnel">
A [Cloudflare Tunnel](/en/setup/cloudflare-tunnel/) is convenient, but Cloudflare's terms of service don't cover serving video through their free network. If you do it anyway, you risk getting your account blocked. For Jellyfin, use a VPN or port forwarding instead.
</Callout>
</Step>

<Step title="Backup">
Back up the `__DATA(jellyfin)__/config` folder. It holds users, settings and what everyone has already watched. You don't need the `cache` folder; Jellyfin rebuilds it. If you back up with [borgmatic](/en/setup/backups-borgmatic/), you can exclude it.

Your media itself lives outside `__ROOT__` and so is **not** in the normal backup. Think about whether you want to back it up: movies you ripped yourself can be ripped again, family videos can't. Those belong in [Immich](/en/services/immich/) or a backup of their own.
</Step>

</Steps>
