title: "Cloudflare Tunnel"
description: "In the end, a service is reachable from the internet under your domain without opening a port on your router. This also works behind CGNAT and DS-Lite."
level: 2
minutes: 20
requires: [Docker Compose, Domain with DNS at Cloudflare]

With a tunnel, your server itself opens an outgoing connection to Cloudflare. Requests to your domain first land at Cloudflare and reach you through this tunnel. Because the connection goes from inside to outside, you need no port forwarding and no public IPv4. The article [Tunnels](/en/wiki/tunnels/) explains how tunnels work in general.

The program on your side is called `cloudflared`. It runs as a container in the proxy network and reaches your services there by their names.

## Pros and cons

| Pros | Cons |
| --- | --- |
| No open port on the router | TLS ends at Cloudflare: Cloudflare can see your traffic unencrypted |
| Works behind CGNAT and DS-Lite | You depend on a US provider and its rules |
| Your home IP stays hidden | On the free plan, uploads are limited to 100 MB per request |
| Free, no server of your own needed | Video streaming (Jellyfin, Plex) isn't covered by the terms of service |

For services like a password manager or photos, the first con means: you trust Cloudflare with your data in plain text. If you don't want that, use a [VPN](/en/setup/vpn-wireguard/) for private services and only use the tunnel for things that are meant to be public anyway. [Access methods compared](/en/wiki/access-methods-compared/) gives an overview.

<Steps>

<Step title="Move your domain to Cloudflare">
The tunnel only works with a domain whose DNS Cloudflare manages. Create a free account at [Cloudflare](https://dash.cloudflare.com/), add your domain and enter the two name servers Cloudflare shows you at your registrar.

It can take a few minutes up to several hours until Cloudflare reports the domain as active. More on DNS in the guide [Domain & DNS](/en/setup/domain-dns/).
</Step>

<Step title="Create the tunnel">
You manage tunnels in the Cloudflare dashboard.

1. Open **Networking → Tunnels** and choose **Create a tunnel**.
2. Give it a name, such as `homelab`, and confirm with **Create Tunnel**.
3. Choose **Docker** as the environment. Cloudflare shows you a `docker run` command.
4. Copy only the long **token** after `--token` from it. You don't need the command itself. Leave the page open; it's waiting for the first connection.

Cloudflare changes its menus from time to time. The current instructions are in the [official documentation](https://developers.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-one/networks/connectors/cloudflare-tunnel/get-started/create-remote-tunnel/).
</Step>

<Step title="Create the folder and .env">
The token is a secret: whoever has it can run your tunnel. So it goes into the `.env`, not into the `compose.yaml`.

```bash
mkdir -p __ROOT__/cloudflared
cd __ROOT__/cloudflared
nano .env
chmod 600 .env
```

Contents of the `.env`:

```bash
TUNNEL_TOKEN=hier-den-token-einfuegen
```

Replace the value with your token.
</Step>

<Step title="Write compose.yaml">
`cloudflared` reads the token from the `TUNNEL_TOKEN` variable. The container sits in the proxy network so it can reach your services by container name. It needs no ports, because it only connects outward.

```yaml
services:
  cloudflared:
    # Version tags are dates (year.month.patch), see hub.docker.com/r/cloudflare/cloudflared
    image: cloudflare/cloudflared:2026.9.3
    container_name: cloudflared
    restart: unless-stopped
    command: tunnel --no-autoupdate run
    environment:
      - TUNNEL_TOKEN=${TUNNEL_TOKEN}
    networks:
      - __NETWORK__

networks:
  __NETWORK__:
    external: true
```
</Step>

<Step title="Start and check">
Start the stack. `cloudflared` connects to Cloudflare right away.

```bash
docker compose up -d
docker compose logs -f cloudflared
```

After a short time, the log should show several lines with `Registered tunnel connection`. Press `Ctrl+C` to leave the view. In the dashboard, the tunnel now shows as **Healthy**, and you can continue with **Continue**.
</Step>

<Step title="Publish a service">
Now tell Cloudflare which name leads to which service. Under **Networking → Tunnels**, open your tunnel, switch to the **Routes** tab and choose **Add route → Published application**. [Uptime Kuma](/en/services/uptime-kuma/) serves as the example:

| Field | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Subdomain | `status` |
| Domain | `__DOMAIN__` |
| Service URL | `http://uptime-kuma:3001` |

For the URL, enter the **container name** and the port **inside the container**. That works because `cloudflared` and the service sit in the same `__NETWORK__` network. Cloudflare creates the matching DNS record itself and takes care of the certificate.

Then open `https://status.__DOMAIN__`. You should see Uptime Kuma. If Cloudflare shows a "Bad Gateway" error instead, usually the container name, port or network is wrong; see [Bad Gateway](/en/wiki/bad-gateway/).
</Step>

<Step title="Secure access">
Everything you publish through the tunnel is public on the internet. The tunnel doesn't replace a login.

- Only publish services that have a good login of their own.
- For everything else, put a login in front. You can do that directly at Cloudflare with **Access** in the Zero Trust dashboard (see the [Cloudflare docs on Access](https://developers.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-one/access-controls/applications/http-apps/self-hosted-public-app/)), or with a service of your own like [Pocket ID](/en/setup/pocket-id/) or [Tinyauth](/en/setup/tinyauth/).
- Don't publish admin interfaces like Portainer or your reverse proxy's interface.
</Step>

</Steps>

<Callout title="Combining the tunnel with Traefik">
Instead of pointing each service at its own container, you can also point the tunnel at Traefik. Then the labels and middlewares from the other guides apply. But there are catches: without open ports, Traefik can't get a certificate through the TLS challenge, and it redirects HTTP to HTTPS. So you have to adjust the tunnel's TLS settings or switch Traefik to the DNS challenge. That's more for advanced users; the options are in the [Cloudflare docs on HTTPS origins behind a tunnel](https://developers.cloudflare.com/tunnel/troubleshooting/https-origins/).
</Callout>
