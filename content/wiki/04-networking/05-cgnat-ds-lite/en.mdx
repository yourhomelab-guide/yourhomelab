## Why this exists

IPv4 addresses are scarce. There are only about 4.3 billion, and they've long been handed out. So internet providers save by having several customers share one public IPv4. For normal browsing, you don't notice. But if you want to make a service at home **reachable from outside**, you have a problem: requests from the internet don't even get as far as your router.

This particularly affects cable and fiber connections, mobile and LTE/5G routers, and some newer DSL contracts.

## CGNAT

Normally your router translates once with NAT between your home network and the internet (see [IP, ports & NAT](/en/wiki/ip-ports-nat/)). With **CGNAT** (carrier-grade NAT), there's a second translation at the provider. Your router then doesn't get a public address but one from a range providers use for this, usually `100.64.0.0/10` (that is, `100.64.x.x` to `100.127.x.x`). Only the provider's big NAT translates your connections to a public IPv4 you share with many others.

Port forwarding in your router then does nothing. The request already gets stuck at the provider, because it doesn't know which of its customers is meant.

## DS-Lite

**DS-Lite** (Dual-Stack Lite) is a common variant of this. Your connection gets a full, public **IPv6**, but no IPv4 of its own. IPv4 traffic is wrapped in IPv6, tunneled to the provider and translated there into the IPv4 internet with CGNAT.

The good news: over IPv6, your connection is directly reachable. The bad news: over IPv4, it isn't. And some networks, for example in hotels, on some mobile plans or in companies, can't do IPv6 yet.

## How to recognize it

Compare two addresses:

1. **The WAN address in the router.** That's the IPv4 the router gets from the provider. You'll find it in the router's interface, usually under overview, internet or online monitor.
2. **Your public IPv4** as the internet sees it. This command shows it, for example:

```bash
curl -4 https://ifconfig.me
```

Then:

| What you see | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Both addresses are the same | You have a public IPv4 of your own. Port forwarding works. |
| Router shows `100.64.x.x` to `100.127.x.x` | CGNAT |
| Router shows a private address like `10.x.x.x` or `192.168.x.x` | Your router sits behind another router or behind CGNAT |
| Router shows no IPv4, but "DS-Lite" or only IPv6 | DS-Lite |

You'll often find hints about DS-Lite directly in the router's interface too. If in doubt, ask your provider whether your connection has a public IPv4 of its own.

<Callout title="A private address in the router isn't necessarily CGNAT">
If your router sits behind a modem or a second router from the provider, you'll also see a private WAN address. Then you can often turn on bridge mode in the front device or set up port forwarding there.
</Callout>

## Ways out

There are several ways to reach your services anyway. Which one fits depends on who needs access.

<Cards>
  <Card title="Ask for a public IPv4">
    Many providers switch you to an IPv4 of your own or real dual stack on request. Sometimes for free, sometimes for a surcharge or only on business plans. A phone call is worth it.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Use IPv6 only">
    With DS-Lite, your server is directly reachable over IPv6. You need an AAAA record and an exception in the router's IPv6 firewall. Only works for devices that have IPv6 themselves.
  </Card>
  <Card title="VPN with NAT traversal">
    Tailscale and similar services build connections from inside to outside and so get through CGNAT. Ideal if only you and your family need access.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Tunnel">
    A service like Cloudflare Tunnel builds an outgoing connection from your server and accepts public requests for you. No open port needed.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Your own VPS as the entrance">
    A small rented server with a fixed IPv4 accepts requests and forwards them through a WireGuard tunnel to your home. More effort, but full control.
  </Card>
</Cards>

A few rules of thumb for choosing:

- **Only you need access?** A VPN is the simplest and safest way. No services have to be public at all. See [VPN](/en/wiki/vpn/).
- **Others should get access without an app**, for example to shared photos? Then a tunnel or a VPS. See [Tunnels](/en/wiki/tunnels/) and [Cloudflare Tunnel](/en/setup/cloudflare-tunnel/).
- **You want it as classic as possible?** Ask your provider for a public IPv4.

A detailed comparison of the options is in [Access methods compared](/en/wiki/access-methods-compared/).

<Callout title="Certificates despite CGNAT">
Even without a reachable server, you get valid certificates: the DNS-01 challenge only checks a DNS record, not your server. See [TLS & certificates](/en/wiki/tls-certificates/).
</Callout>

## In short

- With **CGNAT**, you share a public IPv4 with others, and port forwarding doesn't work.
- **DS-Lite** gives you real IPv6, but no IPv4 of your own.
- To recognize it: compare the WAN address in the router with your public IPv4.
- Ways out: ask your provider for IPv4, IPv6, VPN, tunnel or a VPS as the entrance.
