## Why a roadmap helps

At the start, everything is exciting. You see screenshots of dashboards with twenty services, read about Proxmox clusters and Kubernetes and want everything at once. That's exactly the most common reason homelabs end up back in the closet after a few weeks: too much at the same time, nothing properly understood, and at the first error you don't know where to look.

The roadmap here is deliberately slow. Each week builds on the previous one, and at the end of the month you have a foundation you can keep building on for years. Plan two or three evenings or half a day on the weekend per week. If you need longer, that's completely fine.

Still unsure where to start at all? The [getting-started assistant](/en/tools/getting-started/) asks you a few questions and suggests a suitable path.

## Before you start: one decision

Before the first week begins, settle **where** your server runs: at home, rented or both. [Cloud, hybrid or local?](/en/wiki/cloud-hybrid-local/) and the [hardware overview](/en/wiki/hardware-overview/) help with that. To get started, a used mini PC or an old laptop is often enough. Don't buy anything big yet. You'll only know what you really need after a few months.

## Week 1: the foundation

Goal for this week: a Linux server runs, has a fixed address on your home network, and only you can get in, with an SSH key.

1. [Choosing hardware](/en/setup/choosing-hardware/)
2. [Installing the operating system](/en/setup/installing-the-os/)
3. [Static IP & hostname](/en/setup/static-ip-hostname/)
4. [Setting up SSH keys](/en/setup/ssh-keys/)
5. [Users & sudo](/en/setup/users-sudo/)
6. [Hardening SSH](/en/setup/hardening-ssh/)
7. [Firewall with UFW](/en/setup/firewall-ufw/)
8. [Automatic updates](/en/setup/automatic-updates/)

That sounds like little homelab and lots of chores. But you do these steps exactly once, and afterwards you hardly have to think about the basics anymore.

## Week 2: Docker and the first service

Goal: Docker runs, your folder structure is in place, and a first service is reachable on your home network.

1. [Installing Docker](/en/setup/installing-docker/)
2. [Creating the directory structure](/en/setup/creating-the-directory-structure/)
3. [First stack with Compose](/en/setup/first-stack/)
4. [.env & secrets](/en/setup/env-secrets/)

As your first service, pick something that doesn't hurt if it breaks, for example [Uptime Kuma](/en/services/uptime-kuma/). Start it, stop it, delete the container and start it again. That teaches you that your data survives the container, because it lives in the stack folder. For background reading, [What is Docker?](/en/wiki/what-is-docker/) and [Docker Compose](/en/wiki/docker-compose/) fit well.

## Week 3: access with a domain and HTTPS

Goal: your services have nice names like `status.__DOMAIN__`, a valid certificate, and you can reach them on the go too.

1. [Domain & DNS](/en/setup/domain-dns/)
2. [Reverse proxy with Traefik](/en/setup/reverse-proxy-traefik/) or, if you prefer clicking, [with Nginx Proxy Manager](/en/setup/reverse-proxy-npm/)
3. [VPN with WireGuard](/en/setup/vpn-wireguard/) for access on the go

At the start, open as little as possible to the internet. A VPN is the safest way to begin: your services stay private, and only the VPN entry is reachable from outside. Which way suits your internet line is shown in the [comparison of access methods](/en/wiki/access-methods-compared/). If you don't have a public IPv4 address of your own, read [CGNAT & DS-Lite](/en/wiki/cgnat-ds-lite/) first.

## Week 4: backup, restore and overview

Goal: your homelab folder is backed up every night, you've restored successfully once, and you notice when something goes down.

1. [Backups with borgmatic](/en/setup/backups-borgmatic/)
2. [Testing restores](/en/setup/testing-restores/)
3. [Uptime Kuma](/en/setup/uptime-kuma/) or [Monitoring with Beszel](/en/setup/monitoring-beszel/)
4. [Emergency plan](/en/setup/emergency-plan/): fill in the emergency sheet

<Callout type="warning" title="Backup first, then the important data">
Only move passwords, photos or documents onto the server once your backup runs and you've tried a restore. Before that, your homelab is a playground; after that, it may become important.
</Callout>

Also write down what you've built. The [documentation export tool](/en/tools/docs-export/) takes some of that work off your hands. Why it's worth it is explained under [Documentation](/en/wiki/documentation/).

## What you can leave out for now

A lot is exciting but unnecessary in the first month. None of it is bad. It's just too early.

- **Proxmox, clusters and several servers.** One computer with Linux and Docker is enough for dozens of services. Virtualization pays off once you know what you need it for. More under [Operating models](/en/wiki/operating-models/).
- **Kubernetes.** For a homelab with one server, it brings lots of complexity and hardly any benefits.
- **Single sign-on and CrowdSec.** Login protection with [Pocket ID](/en/setup/pocket-id/) or [Tinyauth](/en/setup/tinyauth/) becomes important as soon as you make services publicly reachable. Until then, the VPN is enough.
- **Automated deployments.** [Portainer or Dockhand](/en/setup/portainer-dockhand/), [Git deployments](/en/setup/git-deployments-forgejo/) and [Renovate](/en/setup/updates-renovate/) are handy when you maintain many stacks. With three services, `docker compose pull` is quicker to explain.
- **Expensive hardware, RAID and 10-gigabit networking.** A RAID doesn't replace a backup, and almost any computer from the last ten years is enough for the first services.
- **Twenty services at once.** Take on one new service per week and get to know it properly.

## A few rules of thumb

- **One change at a time.** If something goes wrong, you'll know exactly what caused it. How to go about it is shown in [Troubleshooting: first steps](/en/wiki/first-steps/).
- **Take notes.** Which commands did you run, what didn't work? Otherwise you won't remember in three months.
- **You don't have to understand everything.** Don't copy commands blindly, but you also don't need to know every detail before moving on. The [glossary](/en/wiki/glossary/) helps with unfamiliar terms.
- **Breaks are allowed.** A server that simply runs for a week is a good sign.
