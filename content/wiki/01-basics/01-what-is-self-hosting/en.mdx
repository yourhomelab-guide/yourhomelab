## The idea in a sentence

You probably use a lot of services today that run on third-party servers: photos in the cloud, passwords in a
Online vault, files stored with a hosting provider. With self-hosting, you install similar software on a
your own server. The data is then stored on your server, and you decide who has access to it.

A **server** is nothing out of the ordinary. It simply refers to a computer that runs around the clock and provides services in the
network. This could be an energy-efficient mini-PC sitting on a shelf, an old laptop, or a rented virtual
Server in a data center.

There is a wide selection of free software. Typical projects for beginners:

**Homelab**: a small, personal IT environment at home for running, learning, and experimenting.

## How this works from a technical standpoint

A typical setup on this page consists of just a few components:

1. **Hardware**: a computer that runs continuously. The devices that are suitable are listed in the
   [Hardware Overview](/en/wiki/hardware-overview/).
2. **Operating system**: usually a lightweight Linux distribution such as Debian or Ubuntu Server, without a graphical user interface. You
   Manage it via [SSH](/en/wiki/ssh-basics/) from your regular computer.
3. **Docker**: Each service runs in its own **container**, an isolated environment that contains everything the
   Software requires. It is described in a short file named `compose.yaml`. More on this in
   [What is Docker?](/en/wiki/what-is-docker/)
4. **Reverse Proxy**: a service that accepts requests and forwards them to the appropriate container. It also ensures that
   HTTPS certificates, so you can use addresses like  `vault.__DOMAIN__`instead of an IP address and port. See
   [Reverse Proxy Explained](/en/wiki/reverse-proxy-explained/).
5. **Backups**: a way to safeguard your data in a different location. Without this, self-hosting is a risk.

All services are located in a shared folder, by default`__ROOT__`. For each service, there is a subfolder containing
`compose.yaml`,  and`.env` `data/`. That way, you'll always know where everything is, and you'll end up backing up exactly one folder.

## Where the server is located

Self-hosting doesn't necessarily mean that the computer is sitting in your living room. There are three models: locally at home,
in the cloud on a rented server, or a combination of both. This article explains which option is right for you
[Cloud, hybrid](/en/wiki/cloud-hybrid-local/), [or on-premises?](/en/wiki/cloud-hybrid-local/)

What matters isn't the location, but control: You choose the software, decide on updates, and know that
where your data is stored.

## What You Should Bring

You don't have to be a pro. The following are helpful:

- **Curiosity and patience.** Not everything works on the first try. Reading error messages is part of the process.
- **A little time.** Setup takes a few evenings, and after that, a few minutes a week are usually enough for updates.
  and a quick look at the system.
- **Terminal Basics.** Copying commands, editing files, changing directories. You'll learn the rest as you go.
- **A sense of responsibility.** You're now your own IT department. If something breaks, there's no support,
  who you can call.

The article ["When Is It Worth It?](/en/wiki/is-it-worth-it/)" honestly explores whether the effort is worth it for you,
with pros and cons.
