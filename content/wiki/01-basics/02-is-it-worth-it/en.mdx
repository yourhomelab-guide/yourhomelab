## The short answer

Self-hosting is worth it if you enjoy tinkering, want to be in control of your own data, and are willing to,
to take care of your system on a regular basis. It's not as worthwhile if you just want to save money or use a service
must be accessible under all circumstances.

## Arguments in favor of this

- **Control over your data.** Photos, passwords, and documents are stored on your device. No service provider analyzes them
  ... suddenly changes the terms or discontinues the service.
- **No subscription trap.** Many cloud services charge monthly, per person, or per unit of storage. Your own server
  The main costs are electricity and, initially, hardware. The exact amount is shown in
  [Costs & Power Consumption](/en/wiki/costs-power/)—it calculates these for your device
  [Electricity calculator](/en/werkzeuge/stromrechner/) off.
- **You'll learn a lot.** Linux, networks, DNS, certificates, containers: This knowledge will also help you in your career or when
  An understanding of how the Internet works.
- **Freedom of choice.** You can combine the software that works best for you and aren't tied to any one ecosystem.
- **Storage is inexpensive.** Large amounts of data, such as photo archives or media files, are often significantly
  cheaper than in the cloud.

## Arguments against it

- **Time required.** Setup takes a few evenings. After that, you'll need time for updates and occasional bug fixes.
  and new versions that do things a little differently than the old ones. Expect to spend about half an hour to an hour per week,
  when everything is going well, and more when it isn't.
- **You're the support person.** If a hard drive fails in the middle of the night or a container stops starting up, you're the one who handles it.
  When others (family, friends) use your services, they depend on you.
- **Availability.** Power outages, router restarts, moving, vacations: A home server isn't as reliable as a
  Data center. That's fine for most consumer services, but not for everything.
- **Security is up to you.** A service you put online will be targeted by automated
  Found by scanners. Updates and a small [attack surface](/en/wiki/attack-surface/) are a must.
- **Hardware and electricity also cost money.** You won't save anything compared to a free service.

## The Most Important Responsibility: Backups

With a cloud provider, someone else makes sure your data survives a hard drive failure. With the
Self-hosting is all about you. That's the point that beginners most often underestimate.

<Callout type="warning" title="No Backup, No Self-Hosting">
If you store photos or passwords only on your server, without backing them up somewhere else, they are
less secure in the cloud than before. Set up backups before you move important data, and test the
Restore at least once.
</Callout>

A tried-and-true rule of thumb is **3-2-1**: three copies of your data, on two different types of media, with one of them stored off-site.

## When It's Best to Skip It

Not every service is equally suitable. In these cases, you should think carefully:

- **Email server.** Technically feasible, but a hassle in practice. Many Internet service providers block outbound port 25 for
  Residential connections—and the dynamic IP addresses associated with them—are often blacklisted. To make sure your emails don't end up in the spam folder,
  Among other things, you'll need valid SPF, DKIM, and DMARC records, a proper reverse DNS record, and a
  A clean IP reputation. And if your server goes down, in the worst-case scenario, important emails won't be delivered. For email
  A reputable provider with its own domain is usually the better choice.
- **Services that must always be running.** When an outage causes real damage, such as to business data or
  For services that others rely on, you'll need more than just a single server at home.
- **The only place to store irreplaceable data.** As long as there is no working backup, keep the originals
  I'd rather have them stay where they are now.
- **If you don't have time.** A server that hasn't received updates for months is a risk. In that case, a
  A well-maintained cloud service is the safer choice.
