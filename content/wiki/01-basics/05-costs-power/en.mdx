## Why Idling Matters

A home lab server does almost nothing most of the time. It waits for requests and syncs a few files every now and then
and performs a backup at night. Therefore, it is not the maximum power that determines the electricity bill, but rather the
Fuel consumption at **idle**.

A device that draws 60 watts under load but operates at 8 watts for 23 hours a day is more energy-efficient than one that, in the
It uses 40 watts at idle. So when comparing models, always check the idle power consumption.

## From Watts to Euros

The math is simple. There are 8,760 hours in a year (24 × 365).

1. **Watts × 8,760 = watt-hours per year**
2. **÷ 1,000 = kilowatt-hours (kWh) per year**
3. **× Electricity price per kWh = Annual cost**

Or, in short: **Watts × 8.76 × electricity price = euros per year.**

<Callout title="Assumptions for the examples">
All examples on this page are based on a rate **of 0.35 € per kWh**. This is just an estimate. Your actual price
It's listed on your electricity bill and may differ significantly from it. Just plug it into the formula.
</Callout>

### Sample calculation: 10 watts

Let's say your mini PC uses an average of 10 watts:

- 10 W × 8,760 h = 87,600 Wh
- 87,600 Wh ÷ 1,000 = **87.6 kWh per year**
- 87.6 kWh × 0.35 € = **about 31 € per year**, or about 2.50 € per month

### Rule of thumb

At 0.35 €/kWh, every watt of continuous power consumption costs about 3** € per year**. This makes it easy to compare devices in your head:

## Measure Instead of Estimate

Manufacturer specifications and figures from forums are only a rough guide. Actual fuel consumption depends on your model, the
It depends on the connected hard drives, the BIOS settings, and the running services.

The easiest way to measure is with a **smart plug** (energy monitor). You plug it in between the outlet and
power supply, and it displays the current power consumption and the total in kWh. You can find these devices at affordable prices at home improvement stores,
Some public libraries and consumer advice centers also lend them out.

Here's how to get a realistic value:

1. Connect the server and let it run normally, with all services.
2. Let the test outlet run for a few days—preferably a whole week.
3. Divide the measured kWh by the number of hours and multiply by 1,000. This gives you the average in watts, including
   Backups and peak loads.

## Additional Costs

Electricity is the largest recurring expense for a home server, but it's not the only one.

### Hardware

One-time payment upon purchase. New mini-PCs and used business computers typically range in price from the lower to
in the mid three-digit range; an old computer you already have costs nothing. Hard drives for large amounts of data are available
if necessary. Over the years, replacement parts may occasionally be needed.

### VPS

As a rough estimate, a small virtual server costs about 4 to 5 € per month; larger packages cost accordingly
more. It either replaces the home server or serves as a public gateway in a hybrid model. Which model is best for
"Which one is right for you? [Cloud, hybrid, or on-premises?](/en/wiki/cloud-hybrid-local/)" asks [Cloud](/en/wiki/cloud-hybrid-local/).

### Backup Storage

At least one copy of your data should be stored off-site. This could be on an external hard drive at a friend's house or a
Cloud storage that costs a monthly fee based on the amount of data. Plan for this from the start; see
[Backup Strategies](/en/wiki/backup-strategies/).
