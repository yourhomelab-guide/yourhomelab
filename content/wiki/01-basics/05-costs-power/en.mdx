## Why idle matters

A homelab server does almost nothing most of the time. It waits for requests, syncs a few files now and then and
makes a backup at night. So for your power bill, maximum power isn't what counts, but consumption at **idle**.

A device that draws 60 watts under load but waits at 8 watts for 23 hours a day is more frugal than one that needs
40 watts at idle. So when comparing, always look at idle consumption.

## From watts to euros

The math is simple. A year has 8,760 hours (24 × 365).

1. **Watts × 8,760 = watt-hours per year**
2. **÷ 1,000 = kilowatt-hours (kWh) per year**
3. **× price per kWh = costs per year**

Or in short: **watts × 8.76 × price per kWh = euros per year.**

<Callout title="Assumption for the examples">
All examples on this page use **€0.35 per kWh**. That's just an assumption. Your actual price is on your power bill
and can differ considerably. Simply plug it into the formula, or let the
[power calculator](/en/tools/power-calculator/) work with your price.
</Callout>

### Example: 10 watts

Say your mini PC draws 10 watts on average:

- 10 W × 8,760 h = 87,600 Wh
- 87,600 Wh ÷ 1,000 = **87.6 kWh per year**
- 87.6 kWh × €0.35 = **about €31 per year**, so roughly €2.50 a month

### Rule of thumb

At €0.35/kWh, every watt of continuous draw costs a good **€3 per year**. That lets you compare devices quickly in
your head:

| Average consumption | kWh per year | Costs per year (at €0.35/kWh) | Typical for                    |
| ------------------- | ------------ | ----------------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| 5 W                 | approx. 44   | approx. €15                   | Raspberry Pi, very frugal mini PC |
| 10 W                | approx. 88   | approx. €31                   | Mini PC of the N100/N150 class |
| 15 W                | approx. 131  | approx. €46                   | Used business mini PC          |
| 30 W                | approx. 263  | approx. €92                   | NAS with hard disks            |
| 50 W                | approx. 438  | approx. €153                  | Old desktop PC                 |

The mapping in the right column is rough. Which devices there are is in the
[hardware overview](/en/wiki/hardware-overview/).

## Measure instead of guessing

Manufacturer figures and values from forums are only a starting point. Real consumption depends on your model, the
connected disks, the BIOS settings and the services running.

The easiest way to measure is a **plug-in power meter**. You put it between the socket and the power supply, and it
shows current consumption and the total in kWh. They're cheap at hardware stores, and some public libraries and
consumer advice centers lend them out too.

This gives you a realistic value:

1. Connect the server and let it run normally, with all services.
2. Leave the power meter in place for a few days, ideally a whole week.
3. Divide the measured kWh by the hours and multiply by 1,000. That's the average in watts, including backups and
   load peaks.

<Callout title="Small adjustments">
Hard disks that are allowed to spin down, a switched-off monitor and unneeded devices on USB ports often save a few
watts. On some computers, a look at the power-saving options in the BIOS helps too.
</Callout>

## Other costs

For a server at home, power is the biggest running cost, but not the only one.

### Hardware

Once, when you buy it. New mini PCs and used business computers are usually in the low to middle three-digit range
depending on configuration; an old computer you already have costs nothing. Hard disks for large amounts of data may
come on top. Over the years, the occasional spare part comes up. So you don't buy too big or too small, the
[hardware overview](/en/wiki/hardware-overview/) helps you choose processor and memory.

### Domain

For addresses of your own like `cloud.__DOMAIN__`, you need a domain. Common endings like `.de` or `.com` cost roughly
€5 to €20 per year. Watch the **renewal** price: some providers lure you in with a cheap first year and then charge
considerably more. More on this in [DNS & domains](/en/wiki/dns-domains/).

### VPS

A small virtual server costs roughly from €4 to €5 a month, larger plans correspondingly more. It either replaces the
server at home or serves as the public entry point in the hybrid model. Which model suits you is covered in
[Cloud, hybrid or local?](/en/wiki/cloud-hybrid-local/).

### Backup storage

At least one copy of your data belongs off-site. That can be an external disk at a friend's place or cloud storage
that costs money every month depending on the amount of data. Plan for it from the start, see
[Backup strategies](/en/wiki/backup-strategies/).

## An example for a whole year

Under this page's assumptions, a typical beginner setup could look like this:

| Item                          | Costs per year (rough) |
| ----------------------------- | ---------------------- |
| Mini PC at 10 W on average    | approx. €31 power      |
| Domain                        | approx. €5–20          |
| Off-site backup storage       | depends on provider and amount of data |

Add the hardware once. Compare that with the subscriptions you want to replace, and you'll quickly see whether
self-hosting pays off for you financially too. More in [Is it worth it?](/en/wiki/is-it-worth-it/).
