## Two questions first

Before you decide on a way, settle two things:

1. **Who needs access?** Only you and your family, or other people too, who simply open a link in the browser?
2. **What can your connection do?** Do you have a public IPv4 address of your own, or are you behind [CGNAT or DS-Lite](/en/wiki/cgnat-ds-lite/)? Without a public IPv4, requests from outside don't reach your router.

The first question decides between **private** (VPN) and **public** (port forwarding, tunnel, VPS). The second decides which public ways work at all.

## The options at a glance

<Cards>
  <Card title="Port forwarding">
    The router forwards ports 80 and 443 to your reverse proxy. Direct and without third parties, but needs a public IPv4.
  </Card>
  <Card title="VPN">
    Your devices connect to your home network, encrypted. Nothing is public; only you get in.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Tunnel">
    Your server connects outward to a provider like Cloudflare, which passes the requests through. No open port needed.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Your own VPS">
    A rented server with a fixed IP is your public entrance, connected to your home server via WireGuard.
  </Card>
</Cards>

## Compared

|                    | Port forwarding                | VPN (WireGuard, Tailscale)          | Tunnel (Cloudflare)              | Your own VPS (WireGuard, Pangolin) |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------ | ----------------------------------- | -------------------------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| **Who gets in**    | Everyone                       | Only your devices                   | Everyone                         | Everyone                           |
| **Security**       | Medium, everything depends on the proxy and the services | High, nothing is public | Medium, home IP stays hidden | Medium, home IP stays hidden      |
| **Effort**         | Low                            | Low (Tailscale) to medium (WireGuard) | Low                            | High, a second server              |
| **Works with CGNAT** | No                           | Tailscale yes, WireGuard itself no  | Yes                              | Yes                                |
| **Dependency**     | None                           | None (WireGuard) or the provider (Tailscale) | Cloudflare              | VPS provider                       |
| **For whom**       | Public IPv4, services for others | Admin access, services only for you | CGNAT, quick start          | CGNAT, full control                |

A few notes on the table:

- **Security** here means how much is visible from outside. Port forwarding, tunnel and VPS make your services public in the same way. How secure that is depends on your reverse proxy, login protection and current updates.
- **WireGuard itself** needs port forwarding for a UDP port and therefore also a public IPv4 or working IPv6.
- **Cloudflare Tunnel** isn't suitable for large uploads and media streaming. Details are under [Tunnels](/en/wiki/tunnels/).

## The usual combination

Most homelabs don't use just one way:

- **Admin interfaces and SSH only via VPN.** Dockhand or Portainer, router, Proxmox and the like are never public.
- **Services for others via port forwarding, tunnel or VPS.** For example a Nextcloud for sharing or Vaultwarden for the family, protected by the reverse proxy and a login.
- **Everything else stays on the home network.** Not every service has to be reachable from outside.
