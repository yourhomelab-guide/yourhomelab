## Why Use a VPN

Many of the services in your home lab are just for your own use: Portainer, the Proxmox web interface, the router interface, and SSH. These services have no place on the open Internet. Still, you want to be able to access them while you're on the go.

That's exactly what a **VPN** (*Virtual Private Network*) is for. Your device establishes an encrypted connection to your home network. After that, it behaves as if it were connected to your Wi-Fi. You access services using their internal addresses, such as `192.168.1.10`.

The key advantage: **Only someone with the right key can get in.** An attacker won't even see a login page to try. With WireGuard, the server doesn't even respond to unauthorized packets.

## The Three Common Approaches

<Cards>
  <Card title="Run WireGuard Yourself">
    A lightweight, modern VPN protocol. Runs on your server; you manage the keys yourself. Requires port forwarding for a UDP port.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Tailscale or Headscale">
    It is based on WireGuard but handles key management and connection establishment. It works without port forwarding, even behind CGNAT.
  </Card>
  <Card title="VPN on the Router">
    Many routers come with a built-in VPN server, such as the FRITZ!Box with WireGuard. Setup is just a click away, with no need for additional software on the server.
  </Card>
</Cards>

### WireGuard itself

[WireGuard](https://www.wireguard.com/) is a VPN protocol built into the Linux kernel. The configuration is simple: Each device has a key pair, and the server knows the public keys of all authorized devices.

To allow external devices to connect to your server, you need to open **a UDP port** on your router (51820 is standard). This requires a public IPv4 address or a functioning IPv6 connection. Unlike with a web interface, this open port is not a security risk: WireGuard ignores all packets without a valid key.

If you'd rather click than edit configuration files, [wg-easy](https://github.com/wg-easy/wg-easy) is a web interface that runs as a container and generates QR codes for the mobile app. The "[VPN with WireGuard"](/en/setup/vpn-wireguard/) guide walks you through the setup.

### Tailscale and Headscale

[Tailscale](https://tailscale.com/) also uses WireGuard, but it handles everything for you. You install the client on each device and log in. Tailscale distributes the keys and finds a route between the devices on its own, using its own relay servers if necessary. That’s why you don’t need **to forward any ports**, and it works even behind CGNAT or DS-Lite.

The catch: Coordination is handled by Tailscale's servers, and you'll need an account there. Your actual data is transmitted in encrypted form between your devices. There's a free plan available for personal use.

If you want to handle coordination yourself, use [Headscale](https://github.com/juanfont/headscale). It’s a free replica of the Tailscale coordination server that the official Tailscale clients work with. However, Headscale must be accessible from the Internet, so it typically runs on a small VPS.

### VPN on the Router

If your router has a built-in VPN server, that's often the fastest way. The FRITZ!Box supports WireGuard in the latest versions of FRITZ!OS, while other routers offer WireGuard or OpenVPN. You set up a connection on the router and scan a QR code with your cell phone.

Drawback: You're limited to the router's feature set and updates. And here, too, you need a publicly accessible address.

## Everything through the tunnel, or just the home network?

With any VPN, you decide which traffic goes through the connection:

- **Home Network Only** (*Split Tunneling*): Only requests to your home network addresses go through the VPN; everything else goes directly to the Internet. This is faster and is sufficient for accessing your services.
- **Everything** (*Full Tunnel*): All of your traffic goes through your home connection. This is handy when using other people's Wi-Fi networks, but it's slower because your home upload speed becomes the bottleneck.

With WireGuard, you can control this in`AllowedIPs` the client configuration:  `192.168.1.0/24`for the home network only,  `0.0.0.0/0`for everything.

<Callout title="No substitute for public services">
A VPN is suitable for services that only you and your family use. If you want to share something with people who shouldn't install a VPN—such as a Nextcloud link or a photo gallery—you'll need public access via [port forwarding](/en/wiki/port-forwarding/) or [a tunnel](/en/wiki/tunnels/).
</Callout>

## Which path is right for you?

- **Public IPv4 addresses and a passion for technology:** WireGuard itself, possibly with wg-easy.
- **CGNAT or DS-Lite, or if you just want it:** Tailscale.
- **Like Tailscale, but without a third-party provider:** Headscale on a VPS.
- **Your router can do it, and you don't want to install anything:** VPN on the router.

You can find a comparison of all access methods under "[Access Methods Compared](/en/wiki/access-methods-compared/)."
