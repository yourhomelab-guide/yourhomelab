## Why a VPN

Many services in a homelab are only for you: Portainer, the Proxmox web interface, the router interface, SSH. These services have no business on the open internet. Still, you want to reach them on the go.

That's exactly what a **VPN** (*virtual private network*) is for. Your device opens an encrypted connection to your home network. After that, it behaves as if it were on your Wi-Fi. You open services at their internal addresses, such as `__SERVER_IP__`.

The decisive advantage: **only someone with a matching key gets in.** An attacker sees no login page to try things on. With WireGuard, the server doesn't even answer unknown packets.

## The three common ways

<Cards>
  <Card title="Run WireGuard yourself">
    A lean, modern VPN protocol. Runs on your server; you manage the keys yourself. Needs port forwarding for a UDP port.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Tailscale or Headscale">
    Built on WireGuard, but takes care of keys and connection setup. Works without port forwarding, even behind CGNAT.
  </Card>
  <Card title="VPN in the router">
    Many routers come with a VPN server, such as the FRITZ!Box with WireGuard. Set up with a few clicks, without extra software on the server.
  </Card>
</Cards>

### WireGuard yourself

[WireGuard](https://www.wireguard.com/) is a VPN protocol built into the Linux kernel. The configuration is short: every device has a key pair, and the server knows the public keys of all allowed devices.

For devices to reach your server from outside, you forward **one UDP port** on the router (51820 is common). That requires a public IPv4 address or working IPv6. Unlike a web interface, this open port is uncritical: WireGuard ignores all packets without a valid key.

If you'd rather click than write configuration files, [wg-easy](https://github.com/wg-easy/wg-easy) offers a web interface that runs as a container and generates QR codes for the phone app. The guide [VPN with WireGuard](/en/setup/vpn-wireguard/) walks you through the setup.

### Tailscale and Headscale

[Tailscale](https://tailscale.com/) also uses WireGuard, but takes the work off your hands. You install the client on every device and log in. Tailscale distributes the keys and finds a way between the devices by itself, through its own relay servers if needed. That's why you need **no port forwarding**, and it also works behind CGNAT or DS-Lite.

The catch: coordination runs through Tailscale's servers, and you need an account there. Your actual data travels encrypted between your devices. There's a free plan for personal use.

If you want to keep coordination in your own hands, use [Headscale](https://github.com/juanfont/headscale). It's a free re-implementation of the Tailscale coordination server that the official Tailscale clients work with. But Headscale itself has to be reachable from the internet, so it typically runs on a small VPS.

### VPN in the router

If your router comes with a VPN server, that's often the quickest way. Current FRITZ!OS versions on the FRITZ!Box support WireGuard; other routers offer WireGuard or OpenVPN. You create a connection in the router and scan a QR code with your phone.

Downside: you're tied to the router's features and updates. And here too, you need a publicly reachable address.

## Everything through the tunnel, or only the home network?

With every VPN, you decide which traffic goes through the connection:

- **Only the home network** (*split tunneling*): only requests to your home network addresses go through the VPN; the rest goes directly to the internet. That's faster and enough to reach your services.
- **Everything** (*full tunnel*): all your traffic runs through your connection at home. Handy on untrusted Wi-Fi, but slower, because your upload at home becomes the bottleneck.

With WireGuard, you control this with `AllowedIPs` in the client configuration: `192.168.1.0/24` for only the home network, `0.0.0.0/0` for everything.

<Callout title="No replacement for public services">
A VPN suits services that only you and your family use. If you want to share something with people who shouldn't have to install a VPN, like a Nextcloud link or a photo gallery, you need a public entrance through [port forwarding](/en/wiki/port-forwarding/) or a [tunnel](/en/wiki/tunnels/).
</Callout>

## Which way fits?

- **Public IPv4 and you enjoy tinkering:** WireGuard yourself, possibly with wg-easy.
- **CGNAT or DS-Lite, or you want it simple:** Tailscale.
- **Like Tailscale, but without a third-party provider:** Headscale on a VPS.
- **Your router can do it and you don't want to install anything:** VPN in the router.

You'll find a comparison of all access methods under [Access methods compared](/en/wiki/access-methods-compared/).
